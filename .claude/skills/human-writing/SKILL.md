# Human writing

## Rules (Human Prompt 2)
- Use active voice. Instead of "The meeting was canceled by management." write "Management canceled the meeting."
- Address readers directly with "you" and "your".
- Be direct and concise. Example: "Call me at 3pm."
- Use simple language. Example: "We need to fix this problem."
- Stay away from fluff. Example: "The project failed."
- Focus on clarity. Example: "Submit your expense report by Friday."
- Vary sentence structures (short, medium, long) to create rhythm.
- Keep a natural, conversational tone. Keep it real. Example: "This approach has problems."
- Avoid marketing language. Not "Our cutting-edge solution delivers unparalleled results." but "Our tool can help you track expenses."
- Simplify grammar. Example: "yeah we can do that tomorrow."
- Avoid AI filler phrases. Not "Let's explore this fascinating opportunity." but "Here's what we know."

## Avoid (important)
- Cliches, jargon, hashtags, semicolons, emojis, asterisks, em dashes.
- Conditional language (could, might, may) when certainty is possible.
- Redundancy and repetition. Remove fluff.

## AI patterns that still slip through (found in a real test, 2026-11-06)
The rules above removed emojis and hashtags, but a test post still read as generic AI because of these. Check every draft for them:
1. Opening with a proverb or cliche ("It's always darkest before the dawn").
2. Three short sentences in a row as a dramatic beat ("Everyone panics. Headlines scream. Friends sell.").
3. The "X isn't Y. It's Z." construction ("Patience isn't boring. It's a strategy.").
4. A closing question that fishes for comments ("What do you do when...?").
5. A generic topic with no personal detail.

## The biggest fix
Rules change the style, not the substance. A post sounds human when it carries something only the writer has: a real number, a specific moment, a decision, a mistake. If the user gives no personal detail, mark the gap as [FILL IN: ...] and ask for the one detail that matters most, instead of inventing one.

## Hebrew
The same rules apply in Hebrew: short plain sentences, direct "את/אתה", no emojis or hashtags, no long dashes, no marketing words, no proverb openings.
